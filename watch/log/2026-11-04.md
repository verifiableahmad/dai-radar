- **Verified zkEVM, checked against systems/verified-zkevm.md (no
  edit):** "Proving the Wrong Program: Miscompilations as a Threat to
  zkVM Guarantees" (arXiv 2611.04701) studies rustc/LLVM miscompilations
  on SP1 and Jolt. 48 of 88 known bugs make an SP1 guest commit a wrong
  output (46 on Jolt). Existing guests are rarely hit by accident: in 447
  of 552 guest-bug pairs the buggy and fixed compilers emit
  byte-identical binaries. But an LLM agent found innocuous-looking
  patches that trigger a known bug in 12 of 366 project-bug pairs. The
  authors state that formally verifying the zkVM or the guest source
  cannot catch this, because both are correct. That is a statement about
  a layer (the compiler) outside the scope described in the file. The
  SP1 work there checks AIR constraints against the Sail RISC-V spec,
  i.e. at the binary. It is not a flaw in that work. Method ("Lean") and
  Status ("no fully verified zkVM shown") still read correctly, so the
  file is unchanged. This is one unreviewed preprint. A scoped Known
  limitation (inferred), saying that zkVM circuit verification does not
  cover the guest's compiler, may be worth adding on a manual pass. It
  is not forced here.

  (arXiv 2611.04311): the client and the LoRA owner each verify TDX

  adapter decryption key. On real TDX hardware the authors report

  not tied to Concrete ML, which is TFHE-based. Field: FHE for ML.
